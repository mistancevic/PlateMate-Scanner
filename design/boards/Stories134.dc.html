DT185The body: The gut
COACH MILAN, 31 OCTOBER 12:45 AND 13:00
The gut starts in the mouth and ends with what lives in the large bowel. It decides how fast a meal arrives, how full it leaves you, and it makes a good part of the body's serotonin. Its own family, as you asked.
WHAT MOVES IT
The mouth: chewing, the first enzyme, taste adapts in about two weeks (Bertino 1982). The oral bacteria turn nitrate into nitric oxide; a strong mouthwash kills them (Bryan 2012). How fast a meal arrives: fat, protein and fibre slow it, the Fi letter (Ludwig 2002). The microbiome: 30 or more plants a week against 10 (McDonald 2018, American Gut). What it makes: short-chain fatty acids and a large share of serotonin (Valdes 2018, BMJ). The fullness messengers, GLP-1 and PYY, raised most by protein and fibre; the hormone named, the drugs never (Batterham 2006; Gibbons 2013).
WHEN IT HELPS, WHEN IT HURTS
The first week of more fibre, added over a week with water. A plate with variety. A bloated evening is usually the meal. A trip with new food. The week after antibiotics: fibre and fermented foods, nothing sold.
YOUR CASE, PER WEEK
Fibre on the 7-day average against the day, and the count of different plants from the plates. The gap is one legume meal a day and ten plants on the shopping list.
THE WELLNESS LINE
No stool or microbiome test, no probiotic named, no condition. With a gut that keeps hurting: your doctor.
SOURCES TO CHECK BEFORE A BUILD
Bertino 1982; Bryan 2012; Ludwig 2002; McDonald 2018; Valdes 2018; Batterham 2006; Gibbons 2013.
