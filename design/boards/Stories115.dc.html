THE STORY
All eight B vitamins join the water-soluble group, in order, each with both names: B1 thiamine, B2 riboflavin, B3 niacin, B5 pantothenic acid, B6 pyridoxine, B7 biotin, B9 folate, B12 cobalamin. Each opens its own page, the same sections as A to K. On every food card the eight sit among the water-soluble rows, against the label's reference. Two are set by how much you eat: EFSA gives B1 and B3 per megajoule, so your day follows today's energy and is marked for you. B3 is counted the EFSA way, niacin equivalents: the niacin plus the tryptophan from protein divided by 60. B9's upper limit is for folic acid added to foods and supplements only. B7: USDA's rows don't report biotin, so its page says so and leaves Where to find it empty. Drawn: the list, a card, B1, B3, B9, B12 and B7; B2, B5 and B6 follow the same page.
