DT187The body: The brain
COACH MILAN, 31 OCTOBER 12:42 AND 13:00
The brain's messengers decide the part of eating the numbers never show: wanting, mood, stress, the feeling after a session. The strictest wellness line in Explorer.
WHAT MOVES IT
Dopamine is wanting, not liking: it rises on the cue, pushed hardest by sweet, salty and fat together, harder after a short night; the tick on Today is a small dose (Berridge and Robinson 2016; Greer 2013). Serotonin: mood, fullness, sleep; made mostly in the gut; a carb meal lets more tryptophan in; melatonin is made from it (Wurtman 1995). Adrenaline and cortisol, the stress pair: right in the morning and a session, wrong at 23:00; stress eating is a mechanism (Adam and Epel 2007). Endorphins after a walk or a session (Boecker 2008). What the claims get wrong: sugar is not a drug; cravings fade when the cue goes (Benton 2010).
WHEN IT HELPS, WHEN IT HURTS
The first weeks of a plan: plan small, tick often. A walk after lunch. A carb dinner on a hard day. A craving at 22:00 is a cue, often after a short night: the planned snack and dinner at 20:00 take it away. The third week of a plan: change one thing, keep the ticks. A stressful day with a skipped lunch: lunch is the fix.
YOUR CASE, PER WEEK
From the plates, the ticks on Today and the sleep habit: two skipped lunches, two late snacks. The gap is lunch, not the evening.
THE WELLNESS LINE
Mood, cravings, habit and sleep; never depression, anxiety, ADHD or any medication; nothing reads how you feel. When a low mood stays: a doctor or a therapist.
SOURCES TO CHECK BEFORE A BUILD
Berridge and Robinson 2016; Greer 2013; Wurtman 1995; Adam and Epel 2007; Boecker 2008; Benton 2010.
