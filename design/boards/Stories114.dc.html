THE STORY
DEK read as the fat-soluble group: D is built, so A, E and K get their pages, the same sections as D and C. Every vitamin gets both its names, under the name: in the member list, on the food cards and on its page. Vitamin A, retinol and beta-carotene from plants; D, D3 cholecalciferol and D2 ergocalciferol; E, tocopherol; K, K1 phylloquinone and K2 menaquinone; C, ascorbic acid; B12, cobalamin; Folate becomes Vitamin B9, folate. The whole B group, all eight with both names, is DT163; the minerals DT164.
WHAT THE NUMBERS SHOW
Vitamin A against 750 µg: beef liver 100 g brings 7761 µg, ten times the day and more retinol than the upper limit of 3000 µg, so the page says once in a while; a carrot 178 %, a handful of spinach 75 %, a red pepper 50 %. Vitamin E against 13 mg: no single portion covers the day; sunflower seeds and almonds, a handful, about 60 %. Vitamin K against 70 µg: spinach 414 %, kale 279 %, broccoli and Brussels sprouts about 200 %; cheddar brings some K2, which EFSA's value doesn't count.
THE NUMBERS, CHECKED
Claims: Regulation (EU) No 432/2012, Annex: A, six claims (legislation.gov.uk copy and the EUR-Lex consolidated text agree); E, one; K, two (copy of OJ L 136 of 25 May 2012). EFSA: A 750 µg RE men, 650 women (2015); preformed A upper limit 3000 µg RE, retinol only, no limit for beta-carotene (2024); E 13 mg men, 11 women as alpha-tocopherol (2015), upper limit 300 mg (2024); K 70 µg as K1 (2017), no upper limit (EFSA overview of upper limits, version 11). Vitamin A counted the EFSA way, 1 µg RE = 1 µg retinol, 6 µg beta-carotene, 12 µg other carotenoids, from USDA's retinol and carotene rows. Foods: USDA SR Legacy rows read on getfoodfacts.com with FDC ids. Fat and beta-carotene: Brown 2004 (7 people); fat and K1: Gijsbers 1996 (5 people), abstracts on PubMed.
ONE FIX FOR THE BUILD
The food cards count vitamin A as USDA does (RAE, beta-carotene at 12 to 1); the page counts it as EFSA does (RE, 6 to 1), so spinach would read 281 µg on its card and 563 µg on the page. The build moves the cards to EFSA's way too, the same as EU labels, so a food has one number everywhere.
OPEN
Sweet potato: its USDA row couldn't be read, so it's left out. Natto's K2 isn't on its USDA row. K and blood thinners: the page stays with food and points to the doctor, as the safety rules say.
