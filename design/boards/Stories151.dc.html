DT204Food names in one language, and three small fixes
TESTER 2, 30 OCTOBER
After Photo of my meal, Spanać was in Serbian and the other three foods in English. The same screen shows a cake icon on Tortellini, a comma and a point on one card, and three red portion lines on one dish.
COACH MILAN, 18:10
The app's language is the main name. The name from the market sits with it, as the original name of the food in that market. Someone with English on screen who travels to Serbia still finds the food on the shelf. When Serbian comes as a language, it swaps: Serbian first, English under it.
1. FOOD NAMES
Foods from the food table take the app's language, English for now. The market's name shows under them on Foods and on the food page as In shops in Serbia, and on the shopping list. On the Plate only the main name. Foods already in a library switch by themselves, unless you renamed them. A food you named yourself keeps your name. A packed product keeps the name printed on the pack. Search finds every name, as now. This reverses one rule of Foods by place (DT136), where Serbia got Serbian names.
2. THE ICON
Tortellini, ravioli, gnocchi and lasagne get the pasta icon. Torte counts only as a word of its own.
3. ONE DECIMAL POINT
While the app is in English, every number uses a point: 5.1 under your 8.2. The phone's language no longer changes it. The decimal comma comes with the languages (DT203).
4. PORTION LINES ON A DISH
A plate read from a photo of one dish shows no portion lines on the dish's parts. The check of the whole plate against your meal stays. A plate you build yourself keeps its portion lines.
